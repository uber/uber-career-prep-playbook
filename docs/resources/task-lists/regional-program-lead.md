## Program Logistics Planning Before Cohort Launches 

Dedicate 4-6 months just to logistics and approvals planning before the cohort launches.

Do NOT move forward with the cohort launch until all these steps are finalized.

- Review Program Resources and Create Program Templates

<!-- -->

- Action:

  - Review program resources so that you can align your program with the goals of your organization or company.

- Guidance:

  - Create the suggested templates as part of the first step so you have a more thorough understanding of the program’s details and how all the program components fit together.

- Considerations:

  - Does your company already have templates/resources in circulation that you can adapt to this program or will you need to start from scratch?

<!-- -->

- Engage your Leadership Team

<!-- -->

- Action:

  - Ensure that you have engaged and received approval from your Leadership Team so that they can support and advocate for your program.

- Guidance:

  - Your Leadership Team are the ones to approve headcount, budget, staffing needs, and volunteer time.

  - Start engaging with stakeholders as early as you can. These initial conversations take time.

  - Build a buffer in your timeline to prepare different proposals/presentations based on the preferred approaches for each stakeholder.

  - Ensure that your Leadership Team includes someone from the Talent Acquisition Team so that you’re aligned on open headcount and placement for your fellows at the end of the cohort.

- Considerations:

  - Did you give yourself enough time to have meaningful conversations with stakeholders before decision deadlines are due?

  - Did you think through the best presentation approach based on who your stakeholder is?

  - Do you have anyone who can attend the stakeholders meetings with you to help advocate for you and your program?

- Confirm Core Program Team

<!-- -->

- Action:

  - Review the Core Program Team roles and responsibilities and determine the headcount and structure of your internal team.

- Guidance:

  - The Core Program Team members are the ones who are responsible for the actual execution of the program. They take the program from initial engagement and buy-in from the Leadership Team all the way through to internship offers at the end of the cohort.

  - The Core Program Team is most easily categorized into the roles listed below, but do not necessarily require individual headcount for each.

  - The following roles can be full-time roles or part-time depending on your cohort size and bandwidth.

  - If you choose to combine or separate any of the roles, be sure to think through the full list of cascading tasks so that the flow of responsibilities is not blocked.

  - Suggested Core Program Team Role Overviews:

    - Regional Program Lead

      - Manages the overall program logistics, budget, comms, and speakers.

    - [Technical Lead](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1bZjTz4Y-AG08SI-ZrzJoEHY38fdAkayT0EDGomSruJQ/edit?tab=t.0)

      - Drives progress on anything relating to the technical portion of the program.

      - They must know how to code and be comfortable presenting technical material.

    - Mentors and Volunteers Lead

      - Supports volunteering needs from beginning to end.

    - [Recruiting Support](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1FsYzveuHPZppASBDmNrdtj0706DXWjAWzqFaAYnqCM8/edit?tab=t.0)

      - Leads initial program promotion and assists with organizing interviews and offers.

    - [Workshop MC](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1qftABZLyVhzsHkeVpdl-U9p2EzOythJsW0dHhWYg8ak/edit?tab=t.0) (Optional)

      - Acts as the host for all workshops and keeps speakers and fellows engaged and on-track.

<!-- -->

- Considerations:

  - Do you have enough headcount to have one person assigned to each role?

  - Is your cohort large enough that it will require one headcount per role?

  - Will your core team members be working part-time or full-time in these roles?

  - Can the people who hold these roles work on separate teams and/or in separate organizations or do you prefer to have the overall program led by one team?

- Confirm Length of the Program

<!-- -->

- Action:

  - Determine the run-time of your cohort.

- Guidance:

  - A program that runs less than 7 weeks apart per workshop (5 total) is challenging for students and for volunteers since they’ll need time to complete:

    - The technical practice assignments by fellows

    - The grading of technical practice assignments by mentors

    - The review of mock interview feedback by mentors with fellows

    - Office hours and and the time to implement feedback

  - Your program will need to end with enough time to conduct internship interviews and to extend offers at the end of the cohort before internships start.

  - Review your company’s requirements around the length of time required for enrollment past any internship date.

    - For example, students must be enrolled for at least a month after completion of their internships at Uber.

  - It’s more difficult to run a cohort through the holidays due to vacation time for volunteers and students.

  - It’s more difficult for volunteers to commit in January due to yearly planning and catching up from projects over the holidays.

- Considerations:

  - What are the start dates of your company’s internships?

  - How many internships does your company offer each year?

  - Will your cohort be able to join either internship if you offer multiple options?

  - Do your cohort start and stop dates align with multiple internship options?

  - Will your cohort span over the holidays?

  - How long will your technical practice assignments be?

  - What is a realistic time frame for your fellows to complete assignments?

  - How often do you want mentors and fellows to meet?

<!-- -->

- Confirm Size of your Cohort

<!-- -->

- Action:

  - Determine the size of your cohort.

- Guidance:

  - Connect with your Recruiting Team to confirm what headcount is available.

  - You will want to confirm that your curriculum is correlated with pass rates at the end of the cohort so starting with a smaller group will result in more ROI for your organization/ company.

  - Start with a cohort of either 8 or 10 students since that size takes into account:

    - That there may be absences during workshops but that you still want engagement from fellows during breakout sessions, etc.

    - A cohort that is too small may put too much pressure on your students to engage and may not be worth all the volunteering time.

    - It breaks into an even number of students if you work in pairs.

    - A cohort that’s larger than 10 students may overwhelm your Core Program Team as you work through first time logistics.

- Considerations:

  - Will the Talent Acquisition Team be able hire all students in your program if all of them pass the internship interviews at the end of the cohort?

  - What percentage of the internship headcount would be taken up by your program if everyone passes the internship interviews? Does the percentage make sense relative to other hiring pipelines/ initiatives?

  - Does it make sense to request more or less headcount?

  - Are there any programs that are similar enough to yours that you can piggyback off of or that you may need to align with for marketing and process purposes?

  - Will you have enough volunteers to sustain your cohort?

<!-- -->

- Confirm the Dates/ Times of your Workshops

<!-- -->

- Action:

  - Work with your Core Program Team to determine the best dates/times to host your workshops.

- Guidance:

  - Full day workshops will require more break time built into the agenda with time for lunch as well.

  - Full days are more tiring for students and the Core Program Team but may be easier to schedule for students, especially if your program is in-person.

  - The decision to have your program span one academic year or split between two determines which student populations to target during the initial recruiting process.

    - For example: Target 2nd or 3rd years if your goal is to to hire interns and/or full-time upon program completion, but only target 1st-2nd years if you’re only hiring for interns.

  - Your program cohort must end before your company’s internship start date

    - Make sure to consider the end date of the program and leave enough time to conduct interviews, offers extended, offer acceptances, and moving timelines

  - The more internships per year that your company offers, the more chances your fellows will have of being placed due to headcount and business needs.

  - Your students may be able to work around missed classes, but you should not require students to miss tests to attend workshops.

    - Hosting workshops during testing weeks will make it harder for your students to complete their technical practice assignments and stay focused for the mock interviews.

- Considerations:

  - Does your team prefer one full-day workshop or a workshop divided into two half days?

  - Will the duration of your program cohort span one or two academic years?

  - What are the start and end dates for internships in your company?

  - How many internships do you offer each year?

  - Are there any recruiting event timelines that you need to align your program dates to?

  - Does your company have any sort of requirements such as no meeting days or required in-office days that you need to work around?

  - Are there any company holidays or events you need to work around due to the availability and engagement of volunteers?

  - Are there any regional holidays or norms you need to take into consideration?

    - Ex: The standard work day in NAMER is 8-5PM

    - Ex: In EMEA, most people go on holiday for the month of August.

  - What are the midterms/ finals dates of your target schools?

  - What are the holiday schedules for your target schools?

- Confirm your Eligibility Criteria

<!-- -->

- Action:

  - Work with the Recruiting Team and stakeholders to determine your eligibility criteria.

- Guidance:

  - Take the time to thoroughly understand what problem you’re trying to solve and who you can help by running this program.

  - Eligibility criteria is region specific so be sure to look into local rules and regulations.

  - Use eligibility criteria to filter candidates at the top of the funnel via “knockout” questions on your application.

  - Be clear on your application with eligibility criteria definitions.

  - Eligibility criteria is the difference between the total number of applications you receive and viable applications that you can actually move forward with.

  - Include any disqualification information such as the use of AI, incorrect naming conventions for submissions, etc.

- Considerations:

  - Demographics:

    - Who makes up your target audience?

    - Are you legally allowed to ask for this information in your application?

    - Is there a way for you to track this information?

  - Concentration/ Major:

    - What roles are your company hiring for?

    - Is the role/ major/ concentration called the same thing at every school you’re targeting?

  - Graduation Month/Year:

    - What month and year do fellows have to graduate by in order to be eligible for internships upon program completion?

    - How many internships does your company run per year?

    - Can students qualify for just one or for both internships if you run multiple per year?

    - Are there any enrollment requirements around the length of time after internships are completed?

  - Location:

    - What are the business needs at the office(s) you are hiring for?

    - Which office location has an open headcount?

    - Will students need to commute to your office for the workshops and/or internships?

  - General Eligibility:

    - Will students be attending internships while in school?

    - Do you offer relocation incentives for students who are not within commuting distance?

    - Can you hire international students based on student visas and working permits?

    - Do you sponsor student visas or work permits?

    - Do you take students who do not attend 4-year universities?

    - Do you take students who attend coding camps?

    - What are the legal policies your company has in place for hiring students?

- Create your Program Curriculum

<!-- -->

- Action:

  - Create your program curriculum based on your company’s definition of success.

- Guidance:

  - Sessions after lunch should be more active to keep students engaged; schedule technical sessions and the more dense sessions toward the beginning of the day when students are not as tired.

  - If you have breakout sessions and activities scattered throughout your agenda, you may not need as many overall breaks given students will have time to interact vs just sitting and listening to a lecture.

  - Include both technical and social skills curriculum to bridge the gap between these two categories.

  - Evenly distribute lecture-style sessions with more interactive ones.

  - Group your workshops into themes so there’s session alignment and less context switching for students to follow.

- Considerations:

  - Are you focusing on one target audience or multiple?

  - Will your curriculum be applicable to multiple target audiences?

  - What’s the total number of hours of instruction you would like to offer?

  - Can you split these hours evenly between all the workshops?

  - Will you include specific engagement events as part of your curriculum outline?

  - What are the key competencies you’d like your fellows to excel in once they are an employee? Are these requirements technical or behavioral?

  - Does your program include sufficient technical curriculum and practice assignments to prepare students to succeed at your company?

  - Will your sessions be learning sessions (lecture style)or will there be activities scattered throughout? Will there be breakout sessions?

  - Is there enough variety in your sessions to keep students engaged during and between workshops?

  - What is happening in the industry that you should address?

  - Are there trends you can identify that will help prepare students for success?

  - Are there any patterns or challenges faced by your fellows that you can address?

- Confirm if your Program is Virtual or In-Person

<!-- -->

- Action:

  - Decide if hosting your program virtually or in-person makes more sense for your company.

- Guidance:

  - Hosting a program virtually, in theory, allows you to reach more students, but this is only possible if your internships are virtual as well, or if you have relocation options for your students.

  - Virtual programs allow for engagement from more members of your company across many different timezones which create greater networks for your students and a larger pool of volunteers to choose from.

  - In-person programs are generally more costly because they usually include:

    - Meals and snacks

    - Access to meeting and presentation rooms

      - Mock interviews should be held in individual rooms

    - Workshop supplies

    - Require volunteers to be In-person

    - Commuting stipends, etc.

  - In-person programs result in more engagement and a sense of belonging and community.

- Considerations:

  - Do you have the budget, space, and personnel support to host workshops in-person?

  - Is your office located in a place that is easily accessible to students and volunteers during workshop hours?

  - Will there be enough volunteers in-person to run your workshop?

- Confirm Program Budget

<!-- -->

- Action:

  - Think through all aspects of your program to see where you’ll likely incur costs and create and maintain a budget tracker.

- Guidance:

  - The following are some examples of costs incurred in a standard program budget:

    - Virtual

      - SWAG

      - Group activities/ ice breakers/ social events during workshops to foster engagement

      - Meal stipends / vouchers

    - Onsite

      - SWAG

      - Group activities/ ice breakers/ social events during workshops to foster engagement

      - Catering

      - Supplies

      - Meeting and presentation rooms

      - Transportation costs

- Considerations:

  - What does your budget look like relative to other programs your company runs?

  - Do you have examples of other program budgets that you can use as leverage?

  - Which key stakeholder is responsible for your budget?

  - Will you need to tailor your program to meet any needs/requests of your budget sponsor?

  - If your program is split between two calendar years, which year will your program be charged to?

  - Depending on your program timeline and spend, if you work with any vendors, does your company’s payment policy cross any fiscal periods?

<!-- -->

- Partner with Volunteer/Mentor Lead to Co-Create Volunteer Plan

<!-- -->

- Action:

  - Think through how many volunteers you need, who you want to target, and how to engage them.

- Guidance:

  - Ideally target at least the same number of technical volunteers as the size of the cohort in order to simultaneously:

    - Conduct technical cohort entrance interviews

    - Conduct technical internship interviews at the end of the cohort

    - Conduct technical mock interviews during workshops

    - Be matched 1x1 for technical mentorship

  - Ideally target at least the same number of volunteers to conduct behavioral cohort entrance interviews simultaneously

  - Engage a large enough set of volunteers (technical and non-technical) so there are replacement volunteers in case someone needs to be swapped out last-minute.

  - Engage volunteers early in their careers so you build a strong network of volunteers and a culture of volunteerism.

  - Do not try to engage the same group of volunteers too often.

  - If you do a larger call for volunteers, be sure that everyone included in the email is eligible to volunteer.

  - Volunteers who have just started their careers may not be the best mentors as they’re still learning and may not be able to answer more complex career guidance or technical questions.

  - Volunteer interviewers should be trained according to company expectations so their interviews most closely mimics actual interviews.

  - Provide recognition for volunteers.

- Considerations:

  - Are you open to having volunteers hold multiple roles?

  - Are there any background requirements that need to be specified for different roles?

  - What time zones are your volunteers in?

<!-- -->

- Create a Communications Plan

<!-- -->

- Action:

  - Create a communications plan that can be shared with your stakeholders and Core Program Team to ensure communication alignment across target audiences and timelines.

- Guidance:

  - Avoid sending too many communications to the same groups of people over a short span of time. This includes communications from other programs as well.

  - People are less likely to open emails with actions on Fridays and Mondays.

    - Send emails with “asks” on Tuesdays around 10:00AM local time.

  - Include a clear due date for your “asks”.

  - Include enough time in your communications to allow for follow ups as needed.

  - Communications should not require readers to click through too many links; all important information should be in the main body of the communication.

  - Use catchy subject lines.

  - Include at Tl;dr ("Too Long; Didn't Read") at the top of your communications.

  - Be direct as possible without being transactional, use storytelling for updates.

- Considerations:

  - Who should send the communication? Who has the most clout?

  - Do you prefer to send the communication from an alias or a personal email?

  - For responses from larger audiences, do you direct them back to one person or an alias?

  - Who should be CC’ed or BCC’ed on your communications?

  - What’s the best format of communication? (Email, Slack, Newsletter, etc.)

## Application Launches 

- Work with Recruiting Lead to confirm applications numbers are tracking according to set target numbers

- Work with Recruiting Lead to Pivot marketing plan as needed to meet target numbers

- Assign applications to application reviewers and answer questions as needed

- Share marketing efforts with alumni slack channel, mentor channel, Eng LinkedIn, etc. based on target audiences

- Check-in with application reviewers and answer questions as needed

- Track progress of application reviewers and keep everyone on track

- Work with Recruiting Lead on a set cadence to review applications with Core Program Team

- Work with TA to confirm final candidates for interview
